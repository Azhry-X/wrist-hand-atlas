B · Carpal Tunnel Prevalence & Natural History + Radial / Ulnar Test AccuracyPROGNOSIS
CTS Prevalence & Natural HistoryAtroshi 1999
General population (n = 2,466) (Atroshi 1999 JAMA) H: median-territory symptoms 14.4% (13.0–15.8); clinically certain CTS 3.8% (3.1–4.6); electrophysiological median neuropathy 4.9% (4.1–5.8); clinically + electrophysiologically confirmed 2.7% (2.1–3.4); 1 in 5 symptomatic people have CTS; blue-collar 3.5% vs white-collar 1.7%; median neuropathy in 18.4% of asymptomatic controls
Untreated CTS (n = 80) (Padua 1998 Ital J Neurol Sci) L: worsening inversely related to severity; about one-third of mild and moderate cases improved; extreme cases never improved
De Quervain, CMC & TFCC AccuracyRadial / Ulnar
WHAT vs Eichhoff (n = 100, ultrasound reference) (Goubau 2014 J Hand Surg Eur) L: WHAT test Sn 0.99, Sp 0.29, PPV 0.95, NPV 0.67 — more accurate than Eichhoff's test (often wrongly called Finkelstein's)
Thumb CMC grind test (Merritt 2010 J Hand Ther) L: κ 0.48; Sn 42% and 53%, Sp 80% and 93% (two testers); LR+ 2.65 and 6.00; LR− 0.59 and 0.62 — confirms but a negative test does not exclude
Ulnar fovea sign (Tay 2007 J Hand Surg Am · Chang 2019 J Hand Surg Asian Pac) M: Sn 95.2%, Sp 86.5% for foveal disruption and / or ulnotriquetral ligament injury · outcomes (L): non-surgical care 54% improved pain; surgery 83%

Persistent Wrist Pain (Prosser 2012)Arthroscopy Cohort
Persistent wrist pain (Prosser 2012 J Hand Ther) L: improved on average by approximately 50% at one year, but most had some pain and disability; duration of pain and baseline PRWHE explained 24% of 1-year PRWHE
