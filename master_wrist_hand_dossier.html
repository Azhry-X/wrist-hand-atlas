B · Suspected Scaphoid Fracture, Acute Carpal Tunnel Syndrome, CRPS & Red-Flag TriageTRIAGE GATE
Suspected Scaphoid Fracture — Anatomical Snuffbox, Rule & ImagingMallee 2020
Epidemiology & exam (SR/MA, 75 studies) (Carpenter 2014 Acad Emerg Med) M: scaphoid = 70% of carpal fractures; occult fracture prevalence 12% to 57%, point estimate 25% pretest probability with non-diagnostic X-ray; only the absence of snuffbox tenderness helps (LR− 0.15)
Anatomical snuffbox & single signs (Mallee 2014 J Hand Surg Am · Parvizi 1998 J Hand Surg Br) M: snuffbox tenderness the most sensitive test; low specificity → many overtreated; combining tests improved post-test probability · pain on thumb movement Sn 69%, Sp 66% (L)
Clinical decision rule (n = 893, 7.6% fractures) (Mallee 2020 Arch Orthop Trauma Surg) M: sex, snuffbox swelling, snuffbox tenderness, painful ulnar deviation, painful axial thumb compression; internal validation Sn 97%, Sp 20%; 15% fewer unnecessary immobilisations and imaging, 50% lower risk of missing a fracture (not externally validated)
Advanced imaging (Bäcker 2020 J Wrist Surg · Mallee 2015 Cochrane) M: X-ray + exam may miss up to 16%; MRI Sn 94.2%, Sp 97.7%; CT 81.5%, 96.0%; ultrasound 81.5%, 77.4% · Cochrane: CT Sn 0.72 (0.36–0.92), Sp 0.99 (0.71–1.00); MRI 0.88 (0.64–0.97), 1.00 (0.38–1.00); bone scan 0.99 (0.69–1.00), 0.86 (0.73–0.94)
Acute CTS, CRPS & Same-Day Red FlagsHarden 2010
Acute carpal tunnel syndrome after DRF (n = 1,189) (Leow 2021 Eur J Orthop Surg Traumatol) L: 51 (4.3%) developed acute CTS; median onset one week (range 1–12); pain out of proportion 13.7%, reduced power 9.8%; 10 of 51 (20%) resolved without intervention; 5 (10%) developed CRPS; type C fractures at higher risk
CRPS — Budapest criteria (113 CRPS vs 47 neuropathic pain) (Harden 2010 Pain) M: IASP criteria Sn 1.00, Sp 0.41; Budapest clinical criteria Sn 0.99, Sp 0.68; Budapest research criteria Sp 0.79
Pyogenic flexor tenosynovitis — Kanavel signs E: fusiform swelling of the digit, flexed resting posture, tenderness along the flexor sheath, pain on passive extension → same-day surgical referral
Poor-outcome factors (Pang 2007 J Bone Joint Surg Am) L: age > 43 years; diabetes, peripheral vascular disease or renal failure; subcutaneous purulence; digital ischaemia; polymicrobial infection
Other emergencies E: compartment syndrome; septic arthritis; high-pressure injection injury; fight bite (clenched-fist knuckle wound); acute CTS after fracture → same-day surgical referral
Medication & Modality Safety: Houghton 2010 (Guideline): electrophysical contraindications (malignancy, active DVT, pregnancy, electronic implants) • Kearns 2019 E: anticoagulants / coagulopathy — caution — possible increased incidence or severity of haematoma and bruising • Wise 2012 M: fluoroquinolones: tendonitis OR 4.3, rupture OR 2.0; stronger with oral glucocorticoids (OR 9.1) and age > 60 (OR 8.3)
TRIAGE DISPOSITION: Cleared — proceed Imaging required (suspected scaphoid / fracture) Urgent referral (acute CTS after fracture / suspected CRPS) Same-day surgical referral (Kanavel / compartment / septic / injection / fight bite)

Rehabilitation After DRFHandoll 2015
Rehabilitation (Cochrane, 26 trials) (Handoll 2015 Cochrane) M: “not enough evidence available to determine the best form of rehabilitation for people with wrist fractures”
Advice + exercise vs no intervention (Kay 2008 Aust J Physiother) M: advice and exercise program gave “some additional benefits over no intervention”
Volar plate — early vs delayed motion (RCT, n = 48) (Laohaprasitiporn 2022 Sci Rep) M: no significant differences in function, radiographs or complications
Manual therapy (SR/MA) (Gutiérrez-Espinoza 2022 J Man Manip Ther) L: exercise + MWM vs exercise at 12 weeks: PRWE −10.2 (p = 0.02), DASH −9.86 (p = 0.0001); manual lymph drainage reduced oedema (e.g. −17.96 ml at 17–21 days)
