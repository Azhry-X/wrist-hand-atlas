A · Carpal Tunnel Syndrome: Exercise, Mobilisation, Neural Mobilisation & Manual Therapy vs SurgeryMANUAL THERAPY
CTS Manual Therapy EvidenceFernández-de-las-Peñas 2015
Exercise & mobilisation (Cochrane, 16 trials) (Page 2012 Cochrane) L: limited, low-quality evidence; decision should be based on clinician's expertise and patient preference
Neural mobilisation (SR/MA, 40 RCTs) (Basson 2017 JOSPT) M: in CTS most clinical outcomes not improved (P > .11), some positive neurophysiological effects
Manual therapy vs surgery (RCT, 120 women) (Fernández-de-las-Peñas 2015 J Pain) H: PT better at 1 and 3 months: mean pain −2.0 (−2.8 to −1.2) and −1.3 (−2.1 to −.6); worst pain −2.9 and −2.0; similar at 6 and 12 months
JOSPT CTS CPG — Manual Therapy (Erickson 2019)JOSPT 2019
Cervical spine & upper extremity (Erickson 2019 JOSPT CTS CPG) (Guideline): “may perform manual therapy, directed at the cervical spine and upper extremity, for individuals with mild to moderate CTS in the short term”
Orthosis + stretching (Erickson 2019 JOSPT CTS CPG) (Guideline): “may use a combined orthotic/stretching program in individuals with mild to moderate CTS who do not have thenar atrophy and have normal 2-point discrimination”
B · Thumb CMC OA Mobilisation & Manual Oedema MobilisationCMC & OEDEMA
Thumb CMC OAMagni 2022
Manual therapy + exercise (RCT, 60 pts aged 70–90) (Villafañe 2013 JOSPT) M: reduced pain vs sham; limited value for pressure pain threshold, pinch and grip
Mobilisation (SR/MA, 8 RCTs) (Magni 2022 Hand Ther) L: pain SMD 0.53 (0.03 to 1), pinch SMD 0.35 (0.03 to 0.7) — statistically but not clinically significant
Hand OedemaMiller 2017
Manual oedema mobilisation (SR, 10 studies) (Miller 2017 J Hand Ther) L: low-to-moderate evidence supports manual oedema mobilisation with standard therapy for problematic hand oedema
Technique E: proximal clearing first, light skin-traction strokes toward the axilla, elevation and active pumping of the digits; re-measure with figure-of-eight / volumetry
C · Dry Needling Safety & Adverse Events SAFETY SCREEN
Adverse event rates: Brady 2014 M: mild AEs 19.18 %; upper risk rate for significant AEs ≤ 0.04 %; bruising 7.55 %, bleeding 4.65 % • Boyce 2020 M: minor AE 36.7 %; major AE < .1 % (prolonged symptom aggravation .03 %, fainting .02 %, forgotten needles .01 %)
Serious risks & screening: Halle 2016 E: pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade, haematoma, infection, CNS injury; filament needles 32–36 gauge (.25–.20 mm) • Kearns 2019 E: anticoagulants / coagulopathy — caution — possible increased incidence or severity of haematoma and bruising
