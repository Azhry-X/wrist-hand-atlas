Fixation TrialsDRAFFT
DRF ≥ 60 years — WRIST trial (Chung 2020 Plast Reconstr Surg) H: 12-month MHQ: volar plate vs external fixation 3 points (97.5% CL −6.0 to 11.5), vs pinning −0.14 (−9.2 to 8.9); at 6 weeks plate better than external fixation by 19, pinning by 11, casting by 7 (p = 0.03); malunion in 48% of casting participants
K-wires vs volar plate (DRAFFT, n = 461) (Costa 2014 BMJ) H: no clinically relevant PRWE difference at 3, 6 or 12 months (−1.3, −4.5 to 1.8; P = 0.40); DASH at 12 months −3.2 (−6.5 to 0.0)
DRAFFT 5-year (Costa 2019 Bone Joint J) H: PRWE 8.3 (wires) vs 11.3 (plate) (−6.99 to 0.99; p = 0.139) — no clinically relevant difference
Rehabilitation After DRFHandoll 2015
Rehabilitation (Cochrane, 26 trials) (Handoll 2015 Cochrane) M: “not enough evidence available to determine the best form of rehabilitation for people with wrist fractures”
Advice + exercise vs no intervention (Kay 2008 Aust J Physiother) M: advice and exercise program gave “some additional benefits over no intervention”
Volar plate — early vs delayed motion (RCT, n = 48) (Laohaprasitiporn 2022 Sci Rep) M: no significant differences in function, radiographs or complications
Manual therapy (SR/MA) (Gutiérrez-Espinoza 2022 J Man Manip Ther) L: exercise + MWM vs exercise at 12 weeks: PRWE −10.2 (p = 0.02), DASH −9.86 (p = 0.0001); manual lymph drainage reduced oedema (e.g. −17.96 ml at 17–21 days)
B · Carpal Tunnel Release, Scaphoid, Flexor Tendon, Thumb CMC, Trigger Finger & CRPS PreventionPOST-OP
Carpal Tunnel Release & Scaphoid WaistPeters 2016
Surgery vs night splint (RCT, n = 176) (Gerritsen 2002 JAMA) H: success at 3 months 80% vs 54% (difference 26%, 12–40%); at 18 months 90% vs 75% (15%, 3–27%)
Surgery vs non-surgical therapy (no denervation) (Jarvik 2009 Lancet) H: “surgery is useful for patients with carpal tunnel syndrome without denervation”
Rehab after release (Cochrane, 22 trials, 1521 pts) (Peters 2016 Cochrane) M: limited, low-quality evidence; decision “should be based on the clinician's expertise, the patient's preferences and the context”
Scaphoid waist — surgery vs cast (SWIFFT, n = 439) (Dias 2020 Lancet) H: no significant PRWE difference at 52 weeks (surgery adjusted mean 11·9, 9·2–14·5)
Tendon, CMC, Trigger Finger & CRPSHand Surgery
Flexor tendon repair (SR, 34 studies) (Starr 2013 J Hand Surg Am) M: early passive motion: rupture 4%, reduced ROM 9%; early active motion: rupture 5%, reduced ROM 6%
Thumb CMC OA surgery (Cochrane, 11 RCTs) (Wajon 2015 Cochrane) L: LRTI vs trapeziectomy alone: pain −2.8 mm (−9.8 to 4.2); adverse events 19 vs 10 per 100 (RR 1.89, 0.96–3.73) — LRTI may add nothing
Trigger finger — release vs injection (RCTs) (Sato 2012 Rheumatology · Zyluk 2011 J Hand Surg Eur) M: percutaneous and open release similar and superior to steroid injection for cure and relapse; percutaneous release lower recurrence than injection
CRPS after wrist fracture — vitamin C (RCT, 416 pts) (Zollinger 2007 J Bone Joint Surg Am) M: CRPS 2.4% vitamin C vs 10.1% placebo (p = 0.002); 500 mg: 1.8% (RR 0.17, 0.04–0.77)
Vitamin C (MA, 3 RCTs) (Evaniew 2015 J Orthop Trauma) M: “fails to demonstrate a significant benefit”
