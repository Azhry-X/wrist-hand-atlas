A · Carpal Tunnel Syndrome: Corticosteroid Injection, Night Splinting & UltrasoundINJECTION & ORTHOSIS
Corticosteroid InjectionHofer 2021
Steroid injection (Cochrane) (Marshall 2007 Cochrane) M: no better than anti-inflammatory + splint at 8 weeks or laser at 6 months; two injections no better than one
Methylprednisolone vs placebo (RCT, n = 111) (Atroshi 2013 Ann Intern Med) H: 10 weeks: symptom score −0.64 (80 mg) and −0.88 (40 mg) vs placebo; no difference at 1 year; surgery within 1 year 73%, 81%, 92% (80 mg, 40 mg, placebo)
Same trial — 5 years (Hofer 2021 JAMA Netw Open) H: symptom-score change vs placebo 0.14 (−0.17 to 0.45) for 80 mg and 0.12 (−0.19 to 0.43) for 40 mg
Splinting & Therapeutic UltrasoundPage 2012
Splinting (Cochrane 2012) (Page 2012 Cochrane) L: night splint vs no treatment: overall improvement at 4 weeks RR 3.86 (2.29–6.51), low quality; neutral vs extension splint RR 2.43 (1.12–5.28), very low quality
Splinting (Cochrane 2023) (Karjalainen 2023 Cochrane) L: 12 weeks may not be better than six weeks; six months may be better than six weeks (low certainty)
Therapeutic ultrasound (Cochrane) (Page 2013 Cochrane) L: one low-quality trial (68 pts): RR 2.36 (1.40 to 3.98) for improvement at 7 weeks; overall poor-quality evidence
B · JOSPT CTS CPG Orthosis, Physical-Agent & Ergonomics Quotes + De Quervain, CMC OA, Trigger Finger & CRPSCPG & EPAs
JOSPT CTS CPG (Erickson 2019)JOSPT 2019
Orthoses & education (Erickson 2019 JOSPT CTS CPG) (Guideline): “Clinicians should recommend a neutral-positioned wrist orthosis worn at night for short-term symptom relief and functional improvement”; “may suggest adjusting wear time to include daytime, symptomatic, or full-time use when night-only use is ineffective”; “may add patient education on pathology, risk identification, symptom self-management, and postures/activities that aggravate symptoms”; “should recommend an orthosis for women experiencing CTS during pregnancy”
Physical agents (Erickson 2019 JOSPT CTS CPG) (Guideline): “may recommend a trial of superficial heat”; “may recommend the application of microwave or shortwave diathermy”; “may offer a trial of interferential current”; “may perform phonophoresis”; “should not use low-level laser therapy or other types of nonlaser light therapy”; “should not use thermal ultrasound”; “should not use iontophoresis”; “should not use or recommend the use of magnets”
Ergonomics (Erickson 2019 JOSPT CTS CPG · O'Connor 2012 Cochrane) (Guideline): “may educate their patients regarding the effects of mouse use on carpal tunnel pressure”; “may recommend keyboards with reduced strike force” · ergonomic equipment: insufficient evidence to determine benefit or harm (L)
De Quervain, Thumb CMC OA, Trigger Finger & CRPSOrthoses & Injection
De Quervain — injection ± thumb spica (RCT) (Ippolito 2020 Hand (N Y)) L: outcomes comparable except radial wrist pain resolution better with injection alone (100% vs 64%)
Thumb CMC OA — splints (SR/MA) (Buhler 2019 Osteoarthritis Cartilage) M: 3–12 months: pain SMD 0.7 (1.04, 0.35), function SMD 0.42 (0.77, 0.08); low-quality evidence; no short-term effect
Thumb base OA — combined care (RCT, n = 204) (Deveza 2021 JAMA Intern Med) H: splint + exercise + topical NSAID + education vs education: FIHOA −1.7 at 6 weeks, −2.4 (−3.5 to −1.3) at 12 weeks; pain −8.6 mm (−15.2 to −2.0)
Trigger finger — steroid injection (Cochrane, 2 RCTs) (Peters-Veluthamaningal 2009 Cochrane) L: steroid + lidocaine superior to lidocaine alone (“silver” level evidence)
CRPS — graded motor imagery / mirror therapy (Moseley 2004 Pain · Méndez-Rebolledo 2017 J Back Musculoskelet Rehabil) L: GMI RCT in chronic CRPS1 (13 pts) effective; SR of 6 trials (171 pts) GMI / mirror therapy improve pain and function in CRPS 1
